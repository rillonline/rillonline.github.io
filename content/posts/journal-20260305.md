I don't think Ihave the courage to pray Psalm 26 myself though I hear it
read every fifth of the month at morning prayer in the 30-day cycle. I
don't think I can meet its claims of righteousness.

What caught my attention this morning was verse 7:

> Singing aloud a song of thanksgiving\\
> and recounting all your wonderful deeds.

Obviously, This is not a whole sentence. It is set in the context of
worship.

I suppose singing hymns at worship might broadly constitute a song of
thanksgiving. I have pondered for some time the idea of recounting all
your wonderful deeds.Take for example Psalm 145:

> One generation shall praise your works to anotherand declare your power.

Or Psalm 22:

> They [descendants] shall come and make known to a part yet unborn\\
> the saving deeds that he [God] has done.

I don't think in those terms. Perhaps the creeds are the closest thing
to a recital of God's activities and the Eucharist is sometimes called
the Great Thanksgiving which retells the story of the Last Supper.

But on a personal level, the circles I inhabit do not make it a practice
to enumerate God's saving deeds.

I once got lost. I knew I had gotten off at the wrong stop and I was
trying to figure out how to get back on track. I ended up walking into a
building. I hoped someone there would tell me where I was and how to get
to where I was going.

A woman asked me if I wanted a cart. It turned out I had wandered into a
grocery store. I explained what my situation was. She put her own cart
aside and walked me to the other side of the shopping mall to my
destination.

I found this to be quite a remarkable event. It took some time for us to
walk and she had to walk back. She explained that someone else had
helped her father in a similar situation. She was paying that kindness
back.

When I related this story to someone else, she said, “Oh, you
encountered an angel.”

Well, in a general way I thought that was true, but not a heavenly
angel. This was just a woman who was pleasant, kind and took the time to
be helpful.

But is my friend right? Should I be retelling this story as one of God's
deeds?

I remember in college commenting to someone how helpful her sister had
been to me. She said, “Oh, she's a Christian.” At the time, I thought
this was negating the sister's helpfulness and diminishing me as well.
Was I just an object of her Christian duty?

Maybe all this is true. That her sister wanted to rack up good works
points. But if the Holy Spirit was motivating her, it was a work of God
to be recounted.

It's just not the way I typically tell a story. I am far more focused on
the kindness of strangers and the decentcy of most people I meet.
