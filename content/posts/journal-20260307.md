My experiment with ProtonDrive has begged the question: Which files
should I save?

An easy answer is, of course, *all of them*. Although likely possible, I
am discovering that saving every file is a lot like saving every object
in my house. Every time I have moved--and I've moved a lot--I try to get
rid of things. So maybe every time I set up a new place to store files,
I should do the same.

I have lived here five years and I find myself packing boxes with items
I haven't used in five years. They will go to a charity shop. I have
found that ST. Vinnie's is just down the street and takes a wide variety
of objects. The biggest thing they don't take is electronics over five
years old. I now have a growing collection of items that need to be
recycled. The dump costs $20 a visit so I will need a lot of stuff that
St. Vinnie's doesn't take before that goes out of the house.

Fortunately, recycling or erasing files is a whole lot easier and I can
do it from the comfort of my own house.

But anyone who has ever looked at a directory of files knows how
painstaking a task it is to sort through them.

- Do I need this writing fragment?
- Did I use it somewhere else?
- Did I go in a different direction in a larger piece?
- Can I do something else with it? Perhaps it is a better direction if I
  ever get around to revising.

There has also been pleasure at going through the directory files. I
come across characters and settings I haven't visited in a while,. I
smile. I am pleased with my work lying dormant here, copied and copied
and copied from directory to directory, from drive to drive.

In this peripatetic exercise, I have started to think how I should
organize what I keep. The directory structures are not serving me well
and need to be reconsidered. And oh, the duplicates? Many many copies of
the same file. What to keep!
