"Scarboro Fair" in folk circles



The lyrics are copyrighted in their modern recorded versions, but the song exists in many traditional variants and is considered a genuine folk ballad with old roots. The core refrain lists four herbs: parsley, sage, rosemary, and thyme. The verses involve a speaker sending impossible tasks to a former lover — make me a shirt without seam or needlework, find me an acre of land between the sea and the sand, that kind of thing. The tasks are so impossible they read as a formal farewell disguised as conditions.

The herb list is in the public domain as a traditional element. The Simon and Garfunkel arrangement and their specific lyric choices are copyrighted, but the underlying ballad predates them considerably and exists in Francis James Child's collection as Child Ballad 2, "The Elfin Knight,"[5] with many regional variants.

For your purposes the herb list and the impossible-task structure are freely usable, and the impossible task motif could do interesting work at a fair where people trade things that can't be gotten anywhere else — including information about coins from a possibly mythological city.

Surely Childs version is in the public domain



Yes, absolutely. Child died in 1896 and his collection was published in the 19th century, fully public domain. The English and Scottish Popular Ballads is freely available through Project Gutenberg and similar archives. The specific lyrics vary across the variants Child collected but they're all yours to quote or adapt freely.

The Simon and Garfunkel version is the only copyright concern and only for their specific arrangement and lyric choices, which differ from the traditional variants anyway.
